## Correction

Per-read deadlines remain 15 seconds. A rotating batch stops admitting another
intent after its 15-second scheduling window, but a started proof receives a
separate finite budget. A run needs at most six reads including event-gap
confirmation and normalization; a submit needs at most four. Defaults are
90 seconds for a run proof and 60 seconds for a submit proof. One stalled read
still stops its attempt after that read's 15 seconds. Parent cancellation
retains precedence; mutation deadlines and initial uncertain observation are
unchanged.

These are elapsed observation budgets: between-read SQLite work and host
scheduling consume the same allowance. Multiplying by six/four reads is a
finite ceiling, not a guarantee that every sequence of individually timely
reads completes under arbitrary host delay. A sufficiently delayed proof
still times out and remains unproven. Local failure bookkeeping uses the
parent context and can finish after the observation allowance expires.

A run batch can admit a final observation extending its 15-second scheduling
window by at most the 90-second run allowance; the analogous submit allowance
is 60 seconds. The serial uncertain/submit/run sequence therefore has roughly
195 seconds of possible observation windows, with local bookkeeping able to
extend total cycle time. These figures are not a complete mailbox-cycle
wall-clock guarantee. Normal fast reads do not consume the whole allowance.

No strict identity, immutable script binding, final job reread, event boundary,
output/teardown check or rotating cursor guard was removed. Stage/category
diagnostics are fixed allowlisted symbols; nested arbitrary errors stay out
of operational logs. Event-gap confirmation retains its original error
sentinel while preserving the underlying error chain for safe classification.
